# Standards and Sources

## Controlled-language foundations

### ASD-STE100 Simplified Technical English

Official site: <https://www.asd-ste100.org/>

The current official site describes ASD-STE100 as a controlled natural language and an international standard for technical documentation. Issue 9 was released on 15 January 2025.

Relevance to SLE:

- governed rules and dictionary;
- controlled terminology;
- change process;
- explicit conformance-oriented writing.

SLE must not copy aerospace-specific rules without linguistic-domain testing.

### Kuhn — Survey and classification of controlled natural languages

Tobias Kuhn. “A Survey and Classification of Controlled Natural Languages.” *Computational Linguistics* 40(1), 2014.

Publisher page: <https://direct.mit.edu/coli/article/40/1/121/1455/A-Survey-and-Classification-of-Controlled-Natural>

Relevance:

- CNL design space;
- distinction between human-oriented and machine-oriented goals;
- design decisions should follow intended purpose.

### Veizaga et al. — Systematic CNL development

“On systematically building a controlled natural language for functional requirements.” *Empirical Software Engineering* 26, 79 (2021).

Open article: <https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8550625/>

Relevance:

- corpus-driven problem identification;
- systematic construction of a domain CNL;
- empirical evaluation and iteration.

### Kuhn — Evaluation of controlled natural languages

Research record: <https://research.vu.nl/en/publications/how-to-evaluate-controlled-natural-languages-2/>

Relevance:

- controlled user experiments;
- understandability must be measured, not assumed.

## Plain-language foundation

### ISO 24495-1:2023

Official page: <https://www.iso.org/standard/78907.html>

Relevance:

- governing principles for plain-language documents;
- broad applicability, including technical and controlled-language contexts.

## Linguistics conventions

### Leipzig Glossing Rules

Official page: <https://www.eva.mpg.de/lingua/resources/glossing-rules.php>

Relevance:

- established conventions for interlinear morpheme-by-morpheme glossing;
- a baseline that SLE should extend rather than replace.

### LSA Journals Style Sheet

Official page: <https://www.lsadc.org/lsa_journals_style_sheet>

Relevance:

- publication conventions for linguistics;
- evidence that SLE must interoperate with existing editorial styles.

### Unified Style Sheet for Linguistics

Reference copy: <https://langsci-press.org/unifiedstylesheet>

Relevance:

- citation and bibliographic consistency;
- not a controlled prose language.

## Linguistic-data interoperability

### Universal Dependencies

Introduction: <https://universaldependencies.org/introduction.html>

Guidelines: <https://universaldependencies.org/guidelines.html>

Relevance:

- cross-linguistically consistent annotation;
- universal inventories with language-specific extensions;
- documented validation and data format.

### Cross-Linguistic Data Formats

Official site: <https://cldf.clld.org/>

Relevance:

- structured exchange of cross-linguistic data;
- separation of data and tools;
- stable semantics and machine-readable formats.

## Source-use rule

These sources inform the development plan. They do not establish that the proposed SLE rules are effective. Effectiveness requires the studies in [[Evaluation Framework]].
