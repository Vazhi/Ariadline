# Participant Sampling and Recruitment Plan v0.1

## Purpose

This plan defines participant coverage for [[Multi-Domain Reader and Author Evaluation Protocol v0.1]].

The plan is not a claim that the eventual sample will represent linguistics as a whole.

## Eligibility

A participant must:

- be an adult under the applicable local rules;
- provide the required consent or authorization;
- be able to complete the assigned language and accessibility format;
- meet the declared role or experience criteria for the assigned stratum;
- not have authored the exact evaluated condition unless participating in a separately labelled author-confirmation task.

Participants may belong to more than one domain. The study records one primary stratum and all relevant secondary experience.

## Primary recruitment strata

### `PG-DESC` — descriptive, documentary, and field linguistics

Includes grammar writers, field researchers, language-documentation researchers, lexicographers, community researchers, and signed-language researchers.

### `PG-THEORY` — theoretical linguistics

Recruit across more than one theoretical or analytical tradition. Do not treat one framework as the neutral default.

### `PG-EMP` — corpus, experimental, phonetic, sociolinguistic, and discourse research

Includes researchers who interpret counts, judgments, measurements, interactional records, or variation data.

### `PG-COMP` — computational linguistics and language resources

Includes system researchers, corpus and treebank developers, annotation designers, and resource maintainers.

### `PG-EDIT` — editors, reviewers, research assistants, and advanced students

Includes people who routinely interpret, revise, adjudicate, or apply linguistic documentation.

## Cross-cutting recruitment requirement

At least 80% of the confirmatory sample must not have contributed to SLE rule, pattern, corpus, checklist, or publication design.

Record but do not use as automatic exclusion:

- career stage;
- primary scholarly language or languages;
- frequency of professional English use;
- subfield and method experience;
- theoretical framework familiarity;
- controlled-language familiarity;
- editing and reviewing experience;
- translation experience;
- accessibility needs;
- relationship to a documented community when relevant and appropriate to record.

## Canto-span participation

Canto-span maintainers or contributors may participate only under a separately labelled flag.

Safeguards:

- they may complete independent general materials when eligible;
- their Canto-span-arm results are reported separately;
- they must not exceed 10% of the pooled confirmatory sample;
- they must not supply more than 25% of any primary stratum;
- no project-wide conclusion may depend on the Canto-span arm.

## Staged sample plan

### Cognitive and operational pilot

Planning target: 24–36 participants.

Minimum coverage:

- at least 4 participants from each primary stratum;
- at least 75% unfamiliar with SLE development;
- no more than 4 Canto-span contributors;
- representation of more than one primary scholarly language;
- at least one participant using a declared accessibility accommodation when recruitment permits.

The pilot tests procedures and estimates nuisance parameters. It does not support the final publication recommendation.

### Confirmatory reader study

Initial planning range: 100–160 analyzable participants.

The exact target must be frozen before main recruitment through a documented simulation or precision analysis using:

- the preregistered primary outcome model;
- the smallest effect of interest;
- item and participant variance estimates from the operational pilot or independent prior data;
- planned domain and condition balance;
- expected attrition.

Minimum quota before any pooled claim:

- at least 20 analyzable participants in each primary stratum;
- at least 80 participants unfamiliar with SLE development;
- no primary condition-by-domain cell with fewer than the frozen minimum;
- no more than 10% Canto-span contributors overall.

The minimum quota is a coverage floor, not a substitute for the frozen sample-size analysis.

### Editorial review study

Planning target: 40–80 analyzable reviewers, including:

- at least 10 experienced editors or reviewers;
- at least 10 research assistants or advanced students;
- domain coverage across all primary strata;
- at least 75% unfamiliar with SLE development.

### Authoring study

Planning target: 40–80 analyzable authors.

Include:

- authors with experience in at least four domain families;
- authors who use English as an additional language;
- authors with no prior SLE experience;
- a separately identified source-author or authorized-proxy subset for authentic meaning confirmation.

### Translation study

Sample size is set by language direction and authority, not pooled across all directions automatically.

Each tested direction should include, where feasible:

- at least 2 translators or bilingual editors;
- at least 8 target-language readers per condition block;
- source-author, authorized-proxy, or domain review for material meaning;
- explicit limits when the available community is small.

Do not pool language directions when terminology, rhetorical order, participant roles, or task design differ materially.

## Recruitment sources

Use multiple independent channels, such as:

- professional associations and subfield lists;
- university departments and research groups;
- journal, volume, corpus, archive, or resource communities;
- translator and editor networks;
- community-research networks with appropriate permission;
- public recruitment posts that state eligibility clearly.

Do not recruit primarily from one repository, institution, social group, or project network.

## Compensation and burden

Compensation, reimbursement, or other recognition must follow local and institutional requirements.

Recruitment materials must state:

- approximate task burden established in the pilot;
- data use and publication plan;
- withdrawal process;
- whether responses may be quoted after de-identification;
- whether specialized or community-controlled material is involved;
- contact and oversight information required locally.

## Accessibility

Before launch:

- provide keyboard-accessible and screen-reader-compatible materials where feasible;
- avoid time limits that invalidate necessary accommodations;
- record accommodated timing separately rather than treating it as poor performance;
- allow breaks for longer authoring or full-document tasks;
- provide terminology and task instructions in accessible formats;
- report unserved access needs as a participant-gap finding.

## Attrition and replacement

- Define completion before recruitment.
- Report starts, withdrawals, exclusions, and analyzable records by stratum and condition.
- Replacement recruitment may fill a preregistered quota without inspecting outcome effects.
- Do not replace a subgroup because its results are unfavorable to SLE.

## Non-generalization rule

A participant result supports only the tested roles, experience ranges, languages, tasks, and materials.

An underrepresented or absent participant group must appear in the participant-gap analysis. It must not be treated as implicitly covered.
