# SLE Kill-Test Decision Matrix v0.1

## Purpose

This matrix prevents a completed test from being interpreted only in ways that preserve the project. It applies to [[Minimal SLE Kill-Test Protocol v0.1]] and the rules in [[SLE Candidate Test Core Register v0.1]].

The matrix is a planning draft. Human statistical and governance review must freeze the exact thresholds before participant data are visible.

## Non-negotiable safety principles

1. A material meaning-preservation failure cannot be offset by average reader benefit.
2. SLE must be compared with competent ordinary expert editing, not only with defective prose.
3. A rule receives no benefit classification when it was not applicable.
4. A package-level benefit cannot hide a harmful or biased rule.
5. `Not determined` remains uncertainty, not partial success.
6. Null, mixed, adverse, and stop results are valid outcomes.
7. Canto-span evidence cannot determine the independent multi-domain result.

## Rule-level actions

| Evidence pattern | Primary action |
|---|---|
| Repeated important benefit over P, preserved meaning, acceptable burden, stable applicability, and no material bias | retain as candidate core control |
| Benefit is confined to one document type, method, or data practice | move to a bounded profile |
| Problem is real but wording, applicability, or exception design causes avoidable failures | revise and retest |
| No important advantage over P and the rule adds burden, repetition, fragmentation, or disagreement | remove from candidate core |
| Any unresolved material meaning change linked to the rule | suspend from candidate core pending repair; remove when repair is not credible |
| Theory, method, language, accessibility, or community bias cannot be narrowed away | remove or transfer only to a legitimately governed local extension |
| Too few applicable passages, unresolved authority, or unstable measurement | insufficient evidence |

## Project-level continuation conditions

Continuing toward a narrowed universal core requires all of the following:

- at least one important primary reader outcome favors S over P or S provides a clearly justified low-burden safety advantage;
- material meaning-preservation failures are not more frequent or more severe in S;
- any retained rule has interpretable rule-level support;
- author and reviewer burden remains acceptable;
- naturalness and cohesion do not show a material adverse pattern;
- repetition and fragmentation do not show a material adverse pattern;
- applicability judgments are sufficiently stable for the claimed scope;
- no unresolved systematic bias is found;
- ordinary expert editing does not already provide the same result at lower burden.

These are decision domains, not frozen numerical thresholds.

## Major reconception conditions

Substantially reconceive SLE as optional editorial guidance, a reporting framework, or a set of domain profiles when one or more of the following occurs:

- only specialized rules show benefit;
- universal rules show little incremental value over P;
- benefits depend mainly on checklists, provenance records, or reporting templates rather than controlled English;
- conformance overhead exceeds the value of the language rules;
- natural prose requires frequent waivers;
- cross-domain applicability is unstable;
- theory-neutrality requires most rules to be locally scoped;
- the strongest result is improved auditability rather than improved reader understanding.

A reconceived project can retain useful artifacts without claiming to be a general simplified language.

## Stop conditions

Stop development of SLE as a formal universal controlled language when approved analysis shows any of the following and no credible narrowing repairs it:

1. S does not outperform P on any important outcome or safety need.
2. S materially changes authorized meaning more often or more severely.
3. S imposes substantially greater editing or review burden without compensating benefit.
4. S produces markedly less natural or cohesive prose.
5. S materially increases repetition or fragmentation.
6. Reviewers cannot apply the candidate rules consistently enough for the claimed use.
7. The controls impose systematic theory, method, language, rhetorical-order, accessibility, or community bias.
8. The project requires so much governance and documentation that ordinary expert editing is the more practical solution.

A stop decision does not require deletion of the repository. Useful checklists, reporting templates, provenance guidance, and study materials may be archived or republished under a narrower description.

## Insufficient-evidence conditions

Use `insufficient evidence` rather than continue, revise, or stop when:

- fewer than the registered number of authentic authorized passages become eligible;
- one or more required subfield strata are missing;
- P or S conditions lack independent preservation review;
- primary outcomes or scoring cannot be applied reliably;
- sample size or missingness prevents the registered inference;
- masking or randomization failure affects interpretation;
- outcome-visible deviations make the planned comparison unreliable.

Insufficient evidence does not support silent retention or stabilization.

## Evidence summary record

For every rule, record:

- applicable passage count;
- P and S preservation results;
- primary and supporting outcomes;
- author and reviewer burden;
- naturalness, cohesion, repetition, and fragmentation;
- applicability agreement;
- adverse and dissent cases;
- domain, theory, language, accessibility, and community scope;
- selected primary action;
- responsible human decision makers;
- date and version of the approved analysis.

For the project, record:

- whether the evidence supports a universal core at all;
- which controls belong in profiles or optional guidance;
- whether the project name and description remain accurate;
- whether another study is justified;
- final conclusion: continue, reconceive, stop, or insufficient evidence.

## Governance boundary

This matrix cannot make a decision automatically. The final action must follow [[Governance and Change Control]], preserve adverse evidence, and record compatibility consequences.

No automated fixture, constructed corpus result, unchecked score, or Canto-span-only finding can trigger a project disposition.
