# Ariadline Kill-Test Decision Matrix v0.1

## Purpose

This matrix prevents a completed test from being interpreted only in ways that preserve the project. It applies to [[Minimal Ariadline Kill-Test Protocol v0.1]] and [[Ariadline Candidate Test Core Register v0.1]].

Human statistical and governance review must freeze exact thresholds before participant outcomes are visible.

## Non-negotiable principles

1. Material meaning-preservation failure cannot be offset by average reader benefit.
2. Ariadline must be compared with competent ordinary expert editing, not only defective prose.
3. A rule receives no benefit classification when it was not applicable.
4. Package-level benefit cannot hide a harmful or biased rule.
5. `Not determined` remains uncertainty.
6. Null, mixed, adverse, and stop results are valid.
7. Canto-span evidence cannot determine the independent multi-domain result.

## Rule-level actions

| Evidence pattern | Primary action |
|---|---|
| Repeated important benefit over P, preserved meaning, acceptable burden, stable applicability, and no material bias | retain as candidate core control |
| Benefit confined to one document type, method, or data practice | move to a bounded profile |
| Problem is real but wording, applicability, or exception design causes avoidable failures | revise and retest |
| No important advantage over P and added burden, repetition, fragmentation, or disagreement | remove from candidate core |
| Unresolved material meaning change linked to the rule | suspend pending repair; remove when repair is not credible |
| Bias cannot be narrowed away | remove or transfer only to a legitimately governed local extension |
| Too few applicable passages, unresolved authority, or unstable measurement | insufficient evidence |

## Continuation conditions

Continuing toward a narrowed universal core requires all of the following:

- at least one important primary outcome favors S over P, or S provides a justified low-burden safety advantage;
- preservation failures are not more frequent or severe in S;
- every retained rule has interpretable rule-level support;
- author and reviewer burden is acceptable;
- naturalness and cohesion show no material adverse pattern;
- repetition and fragmentation show no material adverse pattern;
- applicability judgments are stable enough for the claimed scope;
- no unresolved systematic bias exists;
- ordinary expert editing does not provide the same result at lower burden.

These are decision domains, not frozen numerical thresholds.

## Major reconception conditions

Reconceive Ariadline as optional editorial guidance, a reporting framework, or domain profiles when:

- only specialized rules show benefit;
- universal rules show little incremental value over P;
- benefits come mainly from checklists, provenance records, or templates rather than controlled English;
- conformance overhead exceeds the value of the language rules;
- natural prose requires frequent waivers;
- cross-domain applicability is unstable;
- theory neutrality requires most rules to be local;
- the strongest result is auditability rather than reader understanding.

Useful artifacts may remain without claiming a general simplified language.

## Stop conditions

Stop development of Ariadline as a formal universal controlled language when approved analysis shows any of the following and no credible narrowing repairs it:

1. S does not outperform P on any important outcome or safety need.
2. S changes authorized meaning more often or more severely.
3. S imposes substantially greater burden without compensating benefit.
4. S produces markedly less natural or cohesive prose.
5. S materially increases repetition or fragmentation.
6. Reviewers cannot apply the candidate rules consistently enough.
7. The controls impose systematic theory, method, language, rhetorical-order, accessibility, or community bias.
8. Governance and documentation burden make ordinary expert editing more practical.

A stop decision does not require deleting the repository. Useful checklists, reporting templates, provenance guidance, and study materials may be archived or republished under a narrower description.

## Insufficient-evidence conditions

Use `insufficient evidence` when:

- too few authentic authorized passages become eligible;
- required subfield strata are missing;
- P or S lacks independent preservation review;
- scoring cannot be applied reliably;
- sample size or missingness prevents the registered inference;
- masking or randomization failure affects interpretation;
- outcome-visible deviations make comparison unreliable.

Insufficient evidence does not support silent retention or stabilization.

## Required record

For every rule, record applicable passage count, P/S preservation, primary and supporting outcomes, burden, naturalness, cohesion, repetition, fragmentation, applicability agreement, adverse cases, tested scope, selected action, responsible human decision makers, and approved-analysis version.

For the project, record whether any universal core is supported, which controls belong in profiles, whether the project description remains accurate, whether another study is justified, and the final conclusion.

## Governance boundary

This matrix cannot decide automatically. Final action must follow [[Governance and Change Control]], preserve adverse evidence, and record compatibility consequences.

No automated fixture, constructed corpus result, unchecked score, or Canto-span-only finding can trigger a project disposition.
