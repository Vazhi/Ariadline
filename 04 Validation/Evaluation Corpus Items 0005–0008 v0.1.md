- **Meaning brief:** `SLE-BRIEF-0005`
- **Brief authority:** project-constructed evaluation specification; no external source-author authority
- **Provenance:** project-constructed fictional editorial test item
- **Domain:** typology
- **Method:** cross-language feature comparison
- **Illustrative context:** Illustrative Languages E and F
- **Framework prompt:** descriptive typology
- **Genre:** research summary
- **Pattern:** `SLE-PATTERN-0012`
- **Rules tested:** `SLE-RULE-0003`, `SLE-RULE-0004`, `SLE-RULE-0005`, `SLE-RULE-0006`, `SLE-RULE-0019`, `SLE-RULE-0020`, `SLE-RULE-0022`, `SLE-RULE-0023`

### Authorized meaning brief

> In selected neutral transitive clauses, Languages E and F place the object before the verb. The brief does not authorize identical order across all clause types or a causal claim.

### Uncontrolled draft

> Languages E and F have the same word order because both are SOV languages.

### Proposed controlled alternative

> In the fictional comparison, Languages E and F place the object before the verb in the selected neutral transitive clauses. The comparison does not establish that the languages have identical constituent order in every clause type or discourse context.

### Pair audit

- **Length:** 14 → 39 words (+25); 1 → 2 sentences (+1)
- **Structure and terminology:** Replaces same word order and causal because with a defined comparison property and scope.
- **Uncontrolled-to-brief result:** does not match brief
- **Controlled-to-brief internal result:** matches brief
- **Literal passage-to-passage equivalence:** not equivalent by design when the uncontrolled draft overstates or obscures the authorized brief
- **Independent preservation result:** not determined
- **Risk:** A conventional SOV label may remain useful when the document defines its scope.
