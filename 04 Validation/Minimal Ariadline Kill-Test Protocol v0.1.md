# Minimal Ariadline Kill-Test Protocol v0.1

## Purpose

This protocol asks one bounded question:

> Does the 12-rule candidate core in [[Ariadline Candidate Test Core Register v0.1]] improve important communication outcomes beyond ordinary expert editing without creating unacceptable meaning loss, burden, unnaturalness, repetition, fragmentation, or bias?

It is intentionally smaller than [[Multi-Domain Reader and Author Evaluation Protocol v0.1]]. It is a planning draft, not a frozen protocol or preregistration.

## Authority and prerequisites

Human issues #30 through #35 remain controlling for oversight, permissions, meaning authority, condition development, recruitment, statistical review, execution, and disposition.

Before participant exposure:

- every source must have a legitimate use and authority record;
- every P and S condition must undergo independent meaning-preservation review;
- scoring and adjudication must be frozen;
- accessibility and withdrawal procedures must be approved;
- the analysis plan and sample target must receive human statistical review;
- an immutable registration record must exist.

This note does not satisfy those gates.

## Materials

Use 10 to 12 authentic, authorized passages from at least three substantially different linguistic subfields or practices. Include at least:

1. one descriptive, documentary, fieldwork, or community-governed passage;
2. one theoretical, typological, historical, or argument-focused passage;
3. one corpus, experimental, annotation, resource, or computational passage.

No subfield may contribute more than half of the passages. Canto-span may appear only as a bounded stress test and cannot satisfy independent coverage.

Prefer 100–250 words. Record any justified length exception.

## Passage eligibility

A passage is eligible only when:

- source and version are known;
- permission or another approved lawful basis is recorded;
- a legitimate author, translator, publisher, source, or community authority can define or approve the meaning record;
- at least one candidate-core risk is genuinely present;
- the risk was not created only by truncation or decontextualization;
- protected or identifying information is excluded;
- scoring communication does not require reviewers to settle the underlying linguistic theory.

Record rejected candidates so selection cannot silently favor Ariadline.

## Conditions

### P — ordinary expert editing

A qualified editor receives the passage, authorized meaning record, purpose, readership, and ordinary editorial brief. The editor does not receive the candidate rules. P must represent competent normal practice.

### S — Ariadline candidate-core editing

The legacy condition code `S` is retained for compatibility with the evaluation data model. A comparably qualified editor receives the same information and resources as P, plus the 12-rule candidate core and passage-specific applicability decisions.

S must not receive extra substantive information.

### U — optional source baseline

U is optional and non-primary. Use it only when the unedited source is independently authorized and the task separately registers its purpose. U cannot replace S-versus-P.

## Fairness controls

- Separate P and S editors when practical.
- Match or record expertise, language background, subfield familiarity, and editing time.
- Do not reveal the expected direction of results.
- Give both conditions the same meaning record and source information.
- Allow `not determined` when authorized meaning is insufficient.
- Mask condition labels from readers and scorers.
- Prevent exposure to both P and S for one meaning record.
- Record deviations and information leaks.

## Meaning-preservation gate

A passage-condition pair cannot enter reader testing until independent review covers:

- claim content and scope;
- evidential force;
- uncertainty and modality;
- comparison structure;
- examples and provenance;
- limitations and counterevidence;
- theory- or community-sensitive terminology;
- permissions and access boundaries.

Use `preserved`, `not preserved`, or `not determined`. A material preservation failure excludes the condition from benefit analysis and remains an adverse result.

## Planning sample

The initial planning range is approximately 20–30 readers or editorial reviewers. It is not a justified sample size, frozen quota, recruitment target, or preregistration.

Human statistical review under issue #34 must decide whether the design can answer the registered question.

## Tasks

Use only tasks required by the registered passage risk, such as:

- reconstruct the central claim and scope;
- identify supporting evidence;
- identify a limitation, counterexample, or sensitivity boundary;
- resolve antecedent or logical scope;
- distinguish software behavior from a language claim;
- identify provenance and material transformation;
- rate naturalness, cohesion, repetition, fragmentation, and burden;
- classify rule applicability and outcome.

Do not require every task for every passage.

## Outcomes

The primary publication-relevant contrast is S versus P. Register no more than three primary outcomes before launch. Candidate primary outcomes are:

1. unsupported-inference rate;
2. claim-and-scope reconstruction accuracy;
3. material meaning-preservation failure.

Meaning preservation is a safety outcome and cannot be traded away for reader benefit.

Supporting outcomes include claim-support identification, limitation detection, logical-scope accuracy, evidence-force calibration, editing and response time, naturalness, cohesion, repetition, fragmentation, burden, reviewer agreement, `not determined` frequency, and qualitative bias reports.

## Rule-level analysis

For each rule, report:

- eligible and applicable passage counts;
- P and S editing actions;
- preservation results;
- reader or reviewer outcomes;
- burden and naturalness effects;
- interactions with other rules;
- dissent and adverse cases.

Do not infer rule benefit from the package-level result alone.

## Adversarial checks

Actively search for cases where:

- P solves the problem without Ariadline;
- S adds words without improving interpretation;
- S fragments a cohesive argument;
- S erases deliberate ambiguity or terminological plurality;
- the rule is theory- or method-dependent;
- the meaning record is disputed;
- editors cannot agree that the rule applies;
- community or accessibility requirements conflict with the control.

Retain these cases in the report.

## Decision route

Apply [[Ariadline Kill-Test Decision Matrix v0.1]]. Valid conclusions are:

- continue with a narrowed candidate core;
- move most controls to profiles or optional guidance;
- revise and rerun a smaller test;
- stop or substantially reconceive Ariadline;
- insufficient evidence.

Constructed corpus items and synthetic fixtures can test procedures only. They cannot satisfy authentic-material, reader-benefit, author-safety, or meaning-preservation requirements.
