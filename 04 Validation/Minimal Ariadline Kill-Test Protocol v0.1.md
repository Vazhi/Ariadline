# Minimal Ariadline Kill-Test Protocol v0.1

## Purpose

This protocol asks one bounded question:

> Does the human-approved minimal Ariadline candidate core improve important communication outcomes beyond competent ordinary expert editing without creating unacceptable meaning loss, burden, unnaturalness, repetition, fragmentation, disagreement, or bias?

It is a compact adversarial planning draft, not a frozen protocol, approved study, or preregistration.

## Current issue handoff

The pivoted issue sequence is controlling:

- human issue #40 approves, revises, or rejects the candidate core and project framing;
- human issue #42 acquires authentic passages, lawful-use records, and authorized meaning records;
- this automated issue #43 supplies the compact protocol, templates, scoring structure, data plan, exclusions, and stop routes;
- human issue #44 creates fair P and S conditions and independently reviews preservation;
- automated issue #45 tests the procedure with synthetic records only;
- human issue #46 approves oversight and executes the 20–30-person pilot;
- automated issue #47 analyzes only authorized de-identified data or publishable aggregates;
- human issue #48 selects the evidence-linked project disposition.

The older broad-study issues #9 and #30–#35 remain open or paused source material. This protocol does not close, satisfy, or automatically resume them. Issue #41 remains responsible for any later architecture and migration refactor after #40.

## Human approvals and prerequisites

Before participant exposure:

- #40 must approve an 8–12-rule candidate core;
- #42 must supply 10–12 authentic authorized meaning records;
- #44 must produce fair P/S conditions and independent preservation results;
- #45 must complete the synthetic operational dry run and expose procedural blockers;
- #46 must approve oversight, consent, privacy, accessibility, retention, withdrawal, compensation, recruitment, assignment, data handling, statistical review, and an immutable registration record.

This document satisfies none of those human gates.

## Materials

Use 10–12 authentic, authorized passages from at least three substantially different linguistic subfields, methods, or practices. Include at least:

1. one descriptive, documentary, fieldwork, or community-governed passage;
2. one theoretical, typological, historical, or argument-focused passage;
3. one corpus, experimental, annotation, resource, or computational passage.

No single source, venue, contributor network, theory, language, or subfield may dominate. Canto-span may appear only as a bounded stress test and cannot satisfy independent coverage.

Prefer 100–250 words. Record any justified length exception.

## Passage eligibility and authority

A passage is eligible only when:

- source and exact version are known;
- permission or another approved lawful-use basis is recorded;
- a legitimate author, translator, source, or community meaning authority can approve the meaning record and answer material questions;
- at least one approved candidate-core risk is genuinely present;
- the risk was not created solely by truncation or decontextualization;
- protected or identifying information is excluded or handled under an approved route;
- scoring communication does not require reviewers to settle the underlying linguistic theory.

A publisher or rights holder may confirm publication, version, quotation, or redistribution rights. That role does not automatically authorize the author's or community's intended meaning.

Record rejected candidates and reasons so selection cannot silently favor Ariadline.

## Shared rule-neutral brief

Before editor assignment, freeze one shared packet for P and S. It must include:

- the same source passage and authorized meaning-record versions;
- purpose, intended readership, permitted editing scope, constraints, and allowed resources;
- one rule-neutral description of the authentic communication risk;
- protected terminology, quotations, access boundaries, and information that editors must not add;
- the same timing and `not determined` route.

The shared brief must not contain `SLE-RULE-*` IDs, Ariadline terminology, expected direction, or a condition-specific diagnosis.

An independent coordinator may preregister candidate-rule applicability for later analysis. That mapping is restricted administrative metadata and is not shown to either editor, task designer, reader, scorer, or preservation reviewer before the relevant records are frozen.

## Conditions

### P — ordinary competent expert editing

A qualified editor receives the shared rule-neutral packet and an ordinary professional linguistic-editing brief. The editor does not receive the candidate rules. P must be a credible publication-quality comparator, not an intentionally weak baseline.

### S — minimal Ariadline editing

The legacy code `S` is retained for compatibility. A comparably qualified editor receives the same substantive packet and professional resources as P, plus only the human-approved 8–12-rule candidate core.

The S editor determines and records rule applicability independently and does not receive the coordinator's preregistered mapping. S must not receive extra substantive information.

### U — optional source baseline

U is optional and non-primary. Use it only when the unedited source is independently authorized and the frozen plan states its purpose. U cannot replace S-versus-P.

## Fairness and contamination controls

- Different editors must produce P and S for the same meaning record.
- An editor must not see the other condition's output, action log, unresolved questions, preservation result, reader tasks, or participant results.
- Editors working in both conditions across different passages require frozen counterbalancing and contamination records.
- Match or record expertise, language background, subfield familiarity, editing time, prior Ariadline exposure, and resources.
- Do not reveal the expected direction of results.
- Give both conditions the same meaning record, neutral risk brief, source information, constraints, and resources.
- Allow `not determined` when authorized meaning is insufficient.
- Mask condition labels from readers, scorers, adjudicators, and preservation reviewers.
- Prevent each participant from seeing both P and S for one meaning record.
- Record every information leak and comparability deviation.

## Meaning-preservation gate

A passage-condition pair cannot enter reader testing until independent masked review covers:

- claim content and scope;
- polarity and quantification;
- evidential force, uncertainty, and modality;
- comparison structure;
- examples, citations, and provenance;
- limitations and counterevidence;
- theory-, method-, or community-sensitive terminology;
- permissions and access boundaries;
- normative force where applicable.

Use `preserved`, `not preserved`, or `not determined`.

- Any critical or major non-preservation requires overall `not preserved`.
- Any unresolved material dimension requires overall `not determined`, unless another material dimension already requires `not preserved`.
- Overall `preserved` is permitted only when every applicable material dimension is preserved and every minor or editorial difference is explicitly confirmed nonmaterial.

A material preservation failure excludes the condition from benefit analysis and remains an adverse result. If either member of a P/S pair is `not preserved` or `not determined`, that pair cannot enter the primary benefit comparison.

## Participant assignment

The planning range is approximately 20–30 readers or editors. It is not a justified sample size, recruitment target, or frozen quota.

Use the assignment, counterbalancing, de-identification, exclusion, deviation, and analysis controls in [[Ariadline Kill-Test Data, Assignment, and Analysis Plan v0.1]]. At minimum:

- each participant sees no more than one condition for one meaning record;
- P/S exposure is balanced by material and order as closely as the approved design permits;
- the algorithm, seed, schedule, and hash are frozen before exposure;
- most participants are independent of Ariadline and Canto-span development;
- contributor status and relevant broad experience are recorded without encoding them in participant IDs;
- accessibility accommodations are approved and not treated as inferior performance.

Human issue #46 must approve whether the design can answer the registered question.

## Tasks and scoring

Use only tasks required by the registered rule-neutral passage risk, such as:

- reconstruct the principal claim;
- reconstruct the claim scope;
- identify supporting evidence or a recorded result;
- identify a material limitation, counterexample, or sensitivity boundary;
- identify an unsupported inference or material misinterpretation;
- resolve antecedent or logical scope;
- distinguish software behavior from a language claim;
- identify provenance and material transformation;
- rate naturalness, cohesion, repetition, fragmentation, and burden.

Do not require every task for every passage. Subjective clarity, confidence, or preference ratings do not substitute for scored comprehension.

Task constructs and scoring keys must be derived only from the source, authorized meaning record, shared neutral risk brief, registered construct, and accessibility requirements. Freeze them before task designers or scorers examine P/S outputs, editor logs, condition identities, preservation outcomes, or participant outcomes.

Every scored answer must trace to an authorized meaning record through a frozen question ID and scoring-key version. The scorer-facing key must identify accepted elements, prohibited unsupported elements, partial-credit rules, critical error classes, `not determined` treatment, and adjudication triggers without revealing condition or rule metadata.

## Outcomes

The primary publication-relevant contrast is S versus P. Register no more than three primary outcomes before launch. Candidate primary outcomes include:

1. unsupported-inference or material-misinterpretation rate;
2. principal-claim and claim-scope reconstruction accuracy;
3. material meaning-preservation failure.

Meaning preservation is a safety outcome and cannot be traded away for reader benefit.

Supporting outcomes include evidence identification, limitation detection, logical-scope accuracy, evidence-force calibration, editing and response time, naturalness, cohesion, repetition, fragmentation, burden, scorer agreement, applicability agreement, `not determined` frequency, and qualitative theory, method, language, community, translation, and accessibility concerns.

## Mechanical exclusions and deviations

Freeze exact exclusion codes before exposure. Apply only objective registered rules for consent or eligibility, duplicate participation or exposure, withdrawal, technical failure, frozen missingness requirements, masking or assignment failure, condition comparability, preservation failure, and outcome-visible protocol violations.

Do not exclude disagreement with Ariadline, preference for P, adverse ratings, criticism, uncertainty, `not determined`, null results, or bias reports.

Record every unregistered exclusion or protocol departure in the versioned deviation log defined in [[Ariadline Kill-Test Data, Assignment, and Analysis Plan v0.1]]. Preserve the original frozen protocol and state whether condition-labelled outcomes were visible.

## Small-pilot analysis

The analysis must:

- retain item-level and rule-level results before package conclusions;
- retain P successes and S-created problems;
- report uncertainty, missingness, scorer disagreement, exclusions, and deviations;
- distinguish hard safety failures, descriptive warnings, and insufficient evidence;
- avoid unsupported numerical thresholds and universal population claims;
- avoid inferring rule benefit from package averages when the rule was not applicable;
- preserve null, mixed, harmful, stop, reconception, and insufficient-evidence outcomes.

Use [[Ariadline Kill-Test Decision Matrix v0.1]]. Automated issue #47 may produce a bounded reproducible report only from authorized de-identified data or publishable aggregates. Human issue #48 alone selects the final project disposition.

## Adversarial checks

Actively search for cases where:

- P solves the problem without Ariadline;
- S adds words without improving interpretation;
- S fragments a cohesive argument;
- S erases deliberate ambiguity or terminological plurality;
- a rule is theory-, method-, language-, or genre-dependent;
- an authorized meaning record is disputed or incomplete;
- editors or reviewers cannot agree that a rule applies;
- community, translation, or accessibility requirements conflict with a control;
- governance burden makes competent ordinary editing more practical.

Retain these cases in the report.

## Decision route

Valid conclusions are:

- continue with a bounded candidate core;
- revise or reconceive the project as profiles, optional guidance, checklists, or templates;
- stop Ariadline as a formal controlled language;
- insufficient evidence.

Constructed corpora and synthetic fixtures can test procedures only. They cannot satisfy authentic-material, reader-benefit, author-safety, or meaning-preservation requirements.

## Non-generalization and status boundary

This 10–12-passage, 20–30-person pilot cannot establish that Ariadline represents linguistics as a whole, works across all languages or communities, validates a linguistic analysis, or warrants a universal standard.

This planning document does not approve the core, change [[Evaluation Execution Status v0.1]], authorize recruitment, or advance any human issue.
