# SLE Document Pattern Example Bank v0.1

## Purpose

This bank supplies cross-domain examples for [[SLE for Linguistics Document Patterns v0.1]] and the proposed rules in [[SLE for Linguistics Language Rules v0.1]].

The examples are constructed for editorial evaluation. They are not linguistic evidence and do not establish that the described phenomena or results are real.

A controlled revision is not automatically better. Evaluation must test whether it improves interpretation without changing the author's intended meaning.

# Descriptive grammar

## Uncontrolled

> The prefix is obligatory in subordinate clauses and speakers never omit it.

## Controlled draft

> In the recorded coastal variety, the prefix occurs in all 31 subordinate-clause tokens in Texts 4–9. The current corpus contains no omission. The corpus result does not establish that all speakers require the prefix in every subordinate-clause context.

## Pattern focus

- SLE-PATTERN-0001;
- corpus scope versus language-wide claim;
- attestation versus grammatical requirement;
- bounded negative statement.

# Theoretical syntax or semantics

## Uncontrolled

> Example (22) proves that the quantifier raises at LF.

## Controlled draft

> Under Analysis A, covert quantifier raising derives the reading in Example (22). Analysis B derives the same reading through type shifting. Example (22) is compatible with both analyses and does not distinguish them.

## Pattern focus

- SLE-PATTERN-0003;
- framework-internal derivation versus unique empirical support;
- alternative-analysis visibility.

# Corpus and variation study

## Uncontrolled

> Younger speakers use the innovative form more often.

## Controlled draft

> In Corpus D, speakers aged 18–29 produced 42 innovative tokens in 1,100 eligible contexts. Speakers aged 50–69 produced 18 innovative tokens in 970 eligible contexts. The comparison describes this corpus sample and does not by itself establish a community-wide age change.

## Pattern focus

- SLE-PATTERN-0004;
- comparison items, denominator, and measure;
- sample versus target population;
- result versus interpretation.

# Elicitation or judgment study

## Uncontrolled

> Speakers reject Sentence (8).

## Controlled draft

> In the binary forced-choice task, 17 of 22 eligible participants selected “not acceptable in the stated context” for Sentence (8). Three selected “acceptable,” and two responses were excluded under the preregistered attention criterion.

## Pattern focus

- SLE-PATTERN-0005;
- response task, population, item, and result;
- exclusion visibility;
- task response versus unrestricted speaker judgment.

# Fieldwork note

## Uncontrolled

> The consultant said the verb means “carry.”

## Controlled draft

> During Session F12, Consultant C translated the recorded verb as “carry” after the object-picture prompt. The analyst later normalized the transcription and added the tentative gloss `carry.by.hand`. A follow-up session is needed to test whether the verb excludes carrying on the back.

## Pattern focus

- SLE-PATTERN-0006;
- participant translation versus analyst gloss;
- elicitation context;
- later modification and unresolved boundary.

# Annotation guideline

## Uncontrolled

> Label uncertain tokens `X`, check the context, and send difficult cases to the lead annotator.

## Controlled draft

> If the token cannot be classified from the target sentence, inspect the two preceding and two following sentences. If the context does not resolve the classification, assign `UNCERTAIN`. Send the record to adjudication only when the uncertainty affects a required downstream label.

## Pattern focus

- SLE-PATTERN-0007;
- conditions before actions;
- one principal action per step;
- uncertainty versus negative classification;
- escalation boundary.

# Lexicographic entry

## Uncontrolled

> **flash** — to look quickly; informal; rare.

## Controlled draft

> **flash** *verb*, Sense 3: ‘to look briefly at a person or object and then look away.’ Usage label: informal in the interview corpus. Evidence: 9 tokens from 7 speakers in the 2018–2022 urban-interview subset. The available evidence is insufficient to classify the sense as rare in the wider speech community.

## Pattern focus

- SLE-PATTERN-0008;
- form and sense distinction;
- usage-label scope;
- corpus frequency versus community frequency;
- definition and evidence connection.

# Computational-linguistics system description

## Uncontrolled

> The model understands long-distance agreement because it gets 93% accuracy.

## Controlled draft

> Model M achieved 93% accuracy on Test Set T under Configuration C. Accuracy fell to 61% when agreement cues in the nearest noun phrase conflicted with the target. These results describe model behavior on the stated test sets; they do not establish human-like understanding of long-distance agreement.

## Pattern focus

- SLE-PATTERN-0009;
- system state, input, and configuration;
- performance result versus cognitive or linguistic conclusion;
- error or contrast analysis.

# Language-resource documentation

## Uncontrolled

> The corpus represents the whole language and is free to use.

## Controlled draft

> Release 1.2 contains 84 hours of recorded conversation from three northern communities collected between 2019 and 2023. It does not include ceremonial speech or speakers under 18. Metadata are openly searchable, but access to audio requires community approval under the deposit agreement.

## Pattern focus

- SLE-PATTERN-0010;
- resource coverage versus language coverage;
- access permission versus unrestricted reuse;
- release identity and known omission.

# Research summary

## Uncontrolled

> The study shows that bilingualism improves attention.

## Controlled draft

> In the reported visual-search task, the bilingual group responded 38 milliseconds faster on average than the matched monolingual group. The study did not test attention outside this task, and the confidence interval includes effects smaller than the preregistered practical threshold.

## Pattern focus

- SLE-PATTERN-0012;
- direct result versus broad interpretation;
- task scope;
- material limitation retained in a short summary.

# Limitation and open-question record

## Uncontrolled

> Embedded clauses are a problem and will be fixed later.

## Controlled draft

> The current analysis covers affirmative matrix clauses. It has not been evaluated on embedded clauses. Two embedded examples conflict with the proposed diagnostic, so claims about embedded clauses remain open. Resolution requires a defined embedded-clause sample and review of the diagnostic boundary.

## Pattern focus

- SLE-PATTERN-0013;
- current coverage versus future intention;
- unresolved status versus known falsehood;
- evidence needed for resolution.

# Editorial revision note

## Uncontrolled

> Fixed the definition of *productive*.

## Controlled draft

> The definition of *productive* in Rule Note R7 changed from “occurs with many verbs” to “extends to novel eligible verbs under the stated evaluation method.” This is a normative meaning change, not a typographic correction. Documents that rely only on corpus type counts require review before they can retain the prior conformance declaration.

## Pattern focus

- SLE-PATTERN-0014;
- wording change versus meaning change;
- compatibility effect;
- migration requirement.

# Evaluation use

For each pair, reviewers should record:

1. the claim they understand from each version;
2. the scope they infer;
3. the evidence relation they infer;
4. any lost or added meaning;
5. reading effort;
6. preferred version and reason;
7. whether a third revision is needed.

The example bank must be expanded with authentic, permission-compatible passages before pattern stabilization.
