# Ambiguity and Referential Clarity

## Target ambiguities

The pilot should prioritize ambiguity types that change a linguistic claim:

- pronoun antecedent ambiguity;
- coordination ambiguity;
- modifier attachment ambiguity;
- negation scope;
- quantifier scope;
- comparison-set ambiguity;
- type-token confusion;
- language-data versus analysis confusion;
- term polysemy;
- unmarked changes of population or dataset.

## Referential rule

A referring expression must identify one intended antecedent in the local context.

**Ambiguous**

> The suffix follows the clitic in the corpus, but it is optional.

Possible antecedents: *suffix*, *clitic*, or *order*.

**Controlled**

> The suffix follows the clitic in the corpus. The suffix is optional.

## Deictic expressions

Flag these forms when they do not name a clear object:

- this;
- that;
- these;
- those;
- former;
- latter;
- above;
- below;
- previous;
- following.

## Comparison rule

State the baseline and comparison dimension.

**Ambiguous**

> Form A is more acceptable.

**Controlled**

> Participants gave Form A a higher mean rating than Form B in Task 2.

## Scope rule

Place a scope phrase near the claim that it limits.

**Controlled**

> In the HKCanCor sample, the query returned five tokens.

This sentence does not claim that only five tokens exist in Cantonese.

## Resolution priority

When two readings remain possible, rewrite the sentence. Do not rely only on punctuation or author intention.
