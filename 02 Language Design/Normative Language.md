# Normative Language

## Purpose

[[Normative Language]] gives consistent meanings to requirements, recommendations, permissions, capabilities, and possibilities.

## Proposed normative verbs

| Form | SLE meaning |
|---|---|
| **must** | A requirement for conformance |
| **must not** | A prohibition |
| **should** | A recommendation; a documented reason can justify noncompliance |
| **should not** | A discouraged practice |
| **may** | Permission |
| **can** | Capability or factual possibility, not permission |

## Rule

Do not use *may* and *can* interchangeably in normative text.

## Evidence verbs

The pilot must define an evidence-strength scale.

| Phrase | Intended force |
|---|---|
| **shows** | The stated conclusion follows directly under the declared method and scope |
| **supports** | The evidence increases confidence in the conclusion but is not sufficient alone |
| **suggests** | The evidence gives a preliminary reason to consider the conclusion |
| **is consistent with** | The evidence does not contradict the conclusion, but alternatives remain |
| **does not establish** | The evidence is insufficient for the stated conclusion |
| **contradicts** | The evidence conflicts with a stated prediction or claim under the declared assumptions |

## Restricted phrases

The pilot should flag these phrases for review:

- clearly;
- obviously;
- undoubtedly;
- proves;
- seems;
- appears;
- generally;
- typically;
- commonly;
- often;
- rarely.

These expressions are not always wrong. They require a defined comparison, measure, source, or explicit uncertainty function.

## Quantification

Prefer explicit quantifiers and counts.

**Weak**

> Speakers generally accept the form.

**Better**

> Eight of ten participants rated the form 4 or 5 on the five-point scale.

## Formal proof

Use *prove* only for a formal proof or when a field-specific method justifies that term. Empirical data usually **support**, **show under a defined analysis**, or **fail to reject** a claim.
