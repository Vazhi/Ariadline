# Scope and Non-Goals

## Initial scope

The first release will target English prose used in:

- descriptive and documentary linguistics;
- grammar notes and reference grammars;
- corpus and annotation documentation;
- parser and grammar-engineering documentation;
- methods and evidence reports;
- research summaries for linguists who use English as an additional language.

The initial release will control four layers:

1. [[Grammar and Style Rule Plan|sentence and discourse form]];
2. [[Terminology Control|terminology]];
3. [[Claim-Evidence Matrix|claim and evidence language]];
4. [[Linguistic Examples and Glossing|examples, glosses, and data references]].

## Non-goals

SLE will not initially:

- define a universal linguistic theory;
- require one analysis for disputed phenomena;
- replace the [[Standards and Sources|Leipzig Glossing Rules]];
- replace journal citation or layout styles;
- reduce all linguistic writing to basic English;
- prohibit necessary technical terms;
- guarantee logical formalization;
- determine whether a linguistic claim is true;
- replace peer review;
- standardize every annotation framework.

## Boundary principle

SLE controls **how a claim is written**, not **which theoretical claim a writer must adopt**.

For example, SLE can require a writer to define *subject*. It must not require all theories to use the same definition of *subject*.

## Deferred scope

The following work can begin after the core standard is stable:

- controlled abstracts;
- teaching and public-communication profiles;
- multilingual authoring guidance;
- formal semantic mappings;
- automated rewriting;
- journal or publisher adoption packages;
- domain modules for phonetics, sociolinguistics, psycholinguistics, and NLP.

## Scope test for a proposed rule

A proposed rule is in scope when it improves at least one of these properties:

- interpretation;
- terminological consistency;
- evidence traceability;
- comparison across analyses;
- translation reliability;
- machine-assisted checking.

The rule is out of scope when it primarily enforces taste, school grammar, or one theoretical framework.
